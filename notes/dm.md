# 1.2 Definitions
- DESIGN: as many definitions as designers; "creating a better novel future"; for DM, design must be a PROCESS that can be described and demonstrated.
- MANAGEMENT: "the art of getting things done"; "process of PLANNING, ORGANISING, LEADING and MONITORING the work of organisational members and using all available resources to reach stated goals"; deciding for tomorrow today (vision, strategy, tactics).
- DM: "an INVESTMENT that transforms CREATIVITY into REALITY, delivering better outcomes for all involved."
- For a design MANAGER: EMPOWERING co-designers/co-managers with knowledge, tools and processes so that through design they contribute to the organisation's growth.
- Prof. Pradeep Yammiyavar's definition: managing design-induced processes to achieve 100% transformation of users' NEEDS, WANTS, DESIRES, whose satisfaction leads to PROFITABLE business; taking ideas through stages to implementation within a predefined vision.
- "GOOD DESIGN DOES NOT HAPPEN BY ITSELF — IT HAS TO BE MANAGED" (idea → launch takes years and investment; every decision must be right).
- Design as strategic ASSET: process + resource + accumulated wealth + IP + knowledge equity (trade secret). "Design is SPICE and MEDICINE for innovation" — right dose only. India's creative industry ≈ $30 bn, ~8% of workforce; creative exports +20%, > $11 bn.

# 1.3 Design leader attributes
Articulate & defend design without dominating · bridge visionary ideas and practice · align decisions with user data, show ROI · give the "design voice" (users) weight in meetings · grow self & team · clear steps idea→launch · anticipate disasters, keep alternatives · empower experts on timelines/budgets/tools · connect design vision to mission & goals · delegate by capability · share limelight · ask pointed questions · bend without breaking backs · make others own ideas · common sense · (2026) harness technology/AI for humanity.

# 1.5 Questions DM answers / how a manager is judged
- DM answers: how to show the VALUE of design (metrics); get recognition, credibility, trust for design; build relationships inside the organisation; efficient processes for good output every time; action plan for your and design's position; MONETISE design (creative circular economy).
- Judged by how you manage relationships (organisation–public/stakeholders, organisation–consumers: loyalty, trust, fans; teams; self), processes (project methodology), linking information systems to customer experience through design KNOWLEDGE, and making the organisation FUTURE-SHOCK PROOF (forecasting by analytics). Everyone, even security staff, judges you.

# UNIT 2 — ORGANISATIONS, CORE COMPETENCE, VISION & MISSION (1 Sep)

# 2.1 Organisation and core competency
- ORGANISATION: people sharing interests come together for a PURPOSE and produce together. Like humans: grow or die (infancy → adolescence → maturity → middle age → decline). Growth must be planned and needs a strategy; competitive edge comes from CORE COMPETENCIES; design is becoming a core competency. Types: company, industry, corporate, NGO, govt, institution, private, society, guild, club, professional body, startup.
- CORE COMPETENCY (C.K. Prahalad & Gary Hamel): ability to be especially good at an activity = individual skills + experience harnessed to resources (information, technology).
- 3 tests: (1) hard to IMITATE, (2) REUSABLE across products/markets, (3) adds END-CUSTOMER value.
- Examples: Tata — low-cost efficient manufacturing & design + brand trust + leadership; Apple — hardware–software integration, design & UX, ecosystem, supply chain, brand equity (rivals can't copy the whole); IITG DoD — designers who are technocrats, UI/UX pioneers. Make DESIGN your startup's core competency. Design as mere cosmetics = underselling.

# 2.2 Building blocks, vision, goals, purpose
- Building blocks: MISSION, VISION, GOAL, OBJECTIVE, TARGET, PLAN, STRATEGY, TACTICS.
- Corporate pyramid: Vision → Mission → Objectives → Strategies/Goals/Plans → Programmes (marketing, manufacturing, design). Design manager conceives (down) and implements (up): "talking the walk and walking the talk".
- VISION: aspiration of founders (IITs = "Temples of Learning"); future status and how to reach it.
- GOAL: desired state set earlier; becomes a goal only if action exists and is activated. In design: talk less, demonstrate more. Goal management: recognise team goals, drop irrelevant ones, resolve conflicts, prioritise.
- MISSION ∩ VISION = PURPOSE. Vision: inside→out (push, feeling, passion). Mission: outside→in (pull, sensing, zeal, accomplish). Purpose = reason to exist beyond profit.
- Startups can start envisioning in the middle (goals first). Pyramid: mission → values → vision → strategy → balanced scorecard → implementation → personal plans.

# UNIT 3 — STRATEGY (1–2 Sep)

# 3.1 Strategy, tactics, pyramids
- STRATEGY: plan of action to achieve a goal, motivation "winning" (win-win); "an idea whose hypothesis is that a certain FUTURE POSITION gives ADVANTAGE"; long term; military origin; game theory used. Mantra: where are we now? where do we want to go? how to get there? (more than one way). Contextual; changing too often = incompetent/copied leadership. Confidential road map; communicated via POLICY DOCUMENTS (e.g., National Design Policy).
| Strategy | Tactics |
| Planning / WHY | Doing / HOW |
| Large scale, long term | Small scale, short term, reactionary |
| Difficult to copy | Easy to copy |
- Tactics: Greek "taktike" (organising an army), Chanakya niti; football dodging.
- McGuinness pyramid: PURPOSE (mission, values, vision) → STRATEGY (strategic intent, drivers, enablers) → EXECUTION (targets & initiatives, performance indicators, strategy map).
- Partaker pyramid: vision (why) → mission (what) → core values (how) → strategic priorities (2–5 bets) → long-term goals (3–5 yrs) → initiatives → strategies → tactics → action plan.
- Planning funnel: inputs (values, history, ideas, trends) → mission → market → key success factors → SWOT → assumptions → ideal → key result areas → SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Aggressive, Realistic, Time-based) → strategies → action plans (who/what/when).

# UNIT 4 — BUSINESS PLANNING & STARTUPS (3 Sep)

# 4.1 Plan and business plan
- PLAN: method to move from present to objectives; must be DATA-DRIVEN, TIME-BOUND, MEASURABLE — else not a plan. Corporate planning = whole organisation, long term; business planning = now, a sector, tactical.
- Vision (10–100 yrs: "PC on every desk") → strategy (3–5 yrs: make OS indispensable) → plan (1 yr: force AI into OS till upgrade paid).
- Business plan elements: executive summary (1 page, written LAST), mission, background, product, marketing plan, competitor analysis, SWOT, operations, management, financial plan, timeline; review regularly.
- Process: internal & external situation analysis → SWOT → GAP analysis → resources → WHAT-IF scenarios (designer's visual contribution) → economic forecast → budget & finance modelling.
- Tools: PESTLE (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental); SWOT (internal S/W, external O/T); gap analysis; lean one-page plan (problem, solution, market, revenue, costs, metrics, team, funding, risks); MVP/experiment canvas; product–market fit canvas (value proposition ↔ customer segment).

# 4.2 Startup fundamentals
- MVP: fastest test of a product hypothesis (can be a landing page/ads/sign-up). Traditional wheel→axle→car (unhappy till end) vs MVP skateboard→scooter→bicycle→motorbike→car (happy at each stage).
- Product discovery canvas: vision · users & personas (AARRR: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral) · goals · success measures · timeframe · product stories (story map) · validate (want it? buy it?) · learn (build–measure–learn; pivot or persevere).
- Terms: KPI, USP, ROI, SEM. Competitor SWOT (buy rival product, give to users, observe).

| Team | Weak dynamics → dysfunction |
| Market | No need → no customers |
| Product | Wrong product → poor fit |

# 4.3 Company type, registration, pitching, consultancy
- 3 irreversible decisions: (1) TYPE — Pvt Ltd (scalable, investor-preferred), LLP (flexible, low compliance, no equity funding), OPC (solo, limited liability, full control), partnership/proprietorship (easy, not scalable, ~₹40 lakh ceiling); (2) OWNERSHIP, roles & equity; (3) BRAND identity — name, trademark, domain, handles (register early).

# UNIT 5 — PLC & DATA ANALYTICS (4 Sep)

# 5.1 Product management and PLC
- Product management in DM: product planning & development (R&D, usability engg.), brand management (positioning, image, visual communication), corporate planning (user tracking, competition, strategy, policy), PLC monitoring (feedback, ROI, design audit, documentation). Project life-cycle wheel: planning → concept → detailed design → construction → operations & maintenance → benchmark vs brief.
- PLC stages: introduction → growth → maturity → decline → death/abandonment. Introduction = high cost, NEGATIVE cash flow; profit peaks BEFORE sales (LAG); ROR slope = rate of return.
- Strategies: (1) BY DESIGN — innovation restarts the PLC, or renovate/discount; (2) re-advertise as improved/imported; (3) buy out a competing brand; (4) free service for life/discount; (5) buy one get one (dead inventory). PLC analysis is a full-time job; designers increasingly do it.

# 5.2 Business analytics
- BA: skills, technologies, practices for iterative exploration of past performance (statistics, descriptive & predictive modelling) → insight & planning. Answers: why happening? if trends continue? what next? best possible (optimise)? expectations, future sales, quality, best suppliers/customers, campaigns.
| Type (Gartner) | Question | Output |
| Descriptive | What happened? (hindsight) | Dashboards, reports |
| Diagnostic | Why did it happen? | Analytic dashboard |
| Predictive | What will happen? (insight) | Forecast |
| Prescriptive | How to make it happen? (foresight) | Rules, recommendations — most valuable |
- Data science = maths ∩ technology/hacking ∩ business acumen (designers master business). Designer's unique role: INTERPRET output, SPECIFY VARIABLES, SPOT PATTERNS OF BEHAVIOUR.
- Data mining: statistics on large data to find patterns (collection → selection/exploration → mining → analysis → patterns → interpretation). Uses: risk, retargeting, market-basket/affinity, clustering, competition, profiling, target groups.
- Predictive analytics: unrecognised relationships in structured/unstructured data + external factors → future events (gather → forecast → action). Designers need statistics; Excel (pivot, goal seek, conditional formatting, INDEX-MATCH, waterfall, forecasting).
- DIKW: data → information → skill → knowledge → insight → wisdom. Tools: SAS, R, SPSS, SQL, Excel dashboards. Data on users, competition, company health/performance, politics/policy → investment in innovation.
- Models: linear (relationship), generalised additive (non-parametric), logistic regression (class probability), survival (reliability), decision trees (segment population), Bayesian (conditional probability). ML: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement. Data structures (trees, stacks, arrays, heap, linked list) and algorithms (sort, search, hashing, DP…) → models for predicting.

# UNIT 6 — BRAND MANAGEMENT (7 Sep)

# 6.1 Brand meaning
- Origin: Norse "brandr" = to burn (cattle marks; Egyptian 2000 BC; RFID today). Grew from need for ATTENTION among competitors → STRATEGIC WEAPON; needs design beyond arts & graphics.
- AMA: "name, term, sign, symbol or design, or combination, to identify goods/services and DIFFERENTIATE them from competitors"; Kotler: identifies maker/seller.
- Trademark/name/logo ≠ brand (they are IDENTITIES); brand is abstract; brands have NO EXPIRY (unlike IPR).
| Six levels | Mercedes-Benz |
| Attribute | Well built, engineered, durable |
| Benefits | Functional & emotional gains |
| Culture | German pride (India: royalty, wealth) |
| Personality | No-nonsense BOSS |
| Profile (user) | 55-yr banker, industrialist, politician, star |
| Meta meaning | SUCCESS |
- BRANDS RESIDE IN THE "MENTAL WORLD" OF THE USER. Brand core: vision + image + culture = brand DNA.

# 6.2 Branding, strategy, layers
- BRANDING: creating a distinctive image in users' cognitive realm; brand = mental construct shown through design elements (elements ≠ brand); a process, tool, strategy to elicit behaviour and build long-term relationships. Managing ≠ designing a brand. Mistake: designers think graphics/colour/type = brand.
- Strategy elements: USP, value proposition, segmentation, competitive advantage, positioning statement, target market, perceptual mapping, attributes, emotional appeal.
- Complete brand: STRATEGY (why/who) = substance (purpose, vision, mission, values) + positioning (audience, competition, difference); EXPRESSION (how/what) = persona (voice, personality), communication (messaging, story, tagline), visual (identity, presence). Core / positioning / persona.
- Goals: AWARENESS (recognition, recall, reach → visibility) · TOUCHPOINTS (digital, physical, service → trust) · LOYALTY (satisfaction, connection, rewards → repeat purchase, advocacy).
- Layers (outer → inner): aesthetics (senses) → interaction (behaviour) → function (performance) → construction (reliability) → meaning (TRUST). Revealed layer by layer for memory retention.

# UNIT 7 — CREATIVITY & DESIGN THINKING (8 Sep)

# 7.1 Creativity
- DM role: manage processes and fragile creative people, nurture talent, link creativity to ROI.
- Create (Chambers 1989): bring into being by imagination. For designers: a thinking skill → design CONCEPTS; insight must lead to a PRACTICAL solution.
| Author | Definition |
| Mednick 1964 | New combinations of associative elements that are useful |
| Barron 1961 | New combinations out of the existing |
| Khandwalla 1988 | A state of mind |
| Hurlock 1978 | Personal achievement of uniqueness |
- Output visible, process buried; understood by playful indulgence, metacognition. A measurable form of intelligence. Ideas = ideation; creativity drives innovation; fuels DT's ideation stage.
- Ideas come in a FLASH when relaxed (72% in shower; Kekulé's benzene snake dream; Archimedes' buoyancy) → keep a journal; train the mind. Myths: not academic intelligence; not "crazy" — a human DNA trait in all; neurodivergent think differently.
- ABDUCTIVE thinking; synthesis; left brain (logic, language, numbers, analysis) + right brain (imagination, spatial, music, art) — designers need both (Stroop test). Education curtails creativity.
- Process: AWARENESS → INCUBATION → ILLUMINATION → VERIFICATION (creativity) → IMPLEMENTATION → MARKETING & SELLING (innovation; Kotler). Link governed by commerce.
- MENTAL BLOCK: stuck on old idea; lazy brain (40 W). Techniques: BRAINSTORMING, SCAMPER (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse).
- AI: creates by copying/pixel manipulation; cannot think; output not IPR-protected. Tips: make AI ask YOU questions; teammate not slave; expand imagination; don't follow it; coach/mentor; role-play; skip its first answer; seek hidden pathways; "artificial intuition". Considerations: human intuition + AI, human-centred, privacy/ethics, resistance, AI terms.

[FIG:fig/dm_060_16.jpg|Creativity process: awareness → incubation → illumination → verification → implementation → marketing]
# 7.2 Design thinking
- d.school: EMPATHISE → DEFINE → IDEATE → PROTOTYPE → TEST (Prof. Yammiyavar: textbooks made DT a buzzword). Better: problem space (understand, observe, POV) → solution space (ideate, prototype, test), diverge–converge.
- Characteristics: designerly thinking at each step; human-centred EMPATHY (not sympathy); observing & questioning; CRITICAL thinking to define the real problem; cross-domain ideation by ABDUCTION; hands-on prototyping (fail early); tests to validate. Whole 4-year design curriculum = DT.
- Methods of THINKING (mental) vs DOING (physical). Double Diamond (UK): DIVERGENT (many ideas) → CONVERGENT (best solution). DT = diverge-converge; futures thinking = diverge.
- DT cannot guarantee success. IDEA ≠ CONCEPT (concept = representation of idea); not every idea is creative; idea = SEED of a creative solution.
- Recap of problem solving: awareness (perceptual) → definition → understanding (conceptual) → resource evaluation (INDUCTION) → alternatives (DEDUCTION) → insight (SYNTHESIS, paradigm shift) → choice → verification → conclusion ("designing is celebrating").

# UNIT 8 — INNOVATION (9 Sep)

# 8.1 Innovation vs creativity vs invention
- Innovation = PRACTICAL APPLICATION of creative ideas. Drucker: "purposeful, organised search for CHANGE". Kotler: new ideas → SATISFIED CUSTOMERS. Robinson: applied creativity. Jobs: "saying NO to 1000 things". Sweet spot: desirable ∩ feasible ∩ viable; user = reason. India: NITI Aayog index 2022 (Karnataka #1); GII rank 81 (2015) → 40 (2022).
| Creativity | Innovation |
| Generating ideas, divergent | Implementing, convergent |
| Flash of insight | Step-by-step |
| THOUGHTS | ACTIONS |
- Levitt: creativity = THINKING up new things; innovation = DOING new things. Parallel: need → ideation → invention → innovation → society.
- Invention = new physical device purposefully built (goal-directed); innovation broader (ideas, processes, methods, strategies).
- DESIGN = CREATION (non-existing; thought) + INVENTION (new; implementation) + INNOVATION (novel; improvement). Scientists discover, engineers invent, designers design, everyone innovates. Edison: 1% inspiration, 99% perspiration (inspiration decides quality). "Think with hands, do with minds."

# 8.2 Innovation frameworks & types
- DT (users–technology–market), IDEO (people/desirability, business/viability, technology/feasibility).
| Innovation matrix | Domain not defined | Domain defined |
| Problem defined | BREAKTHROUGH (P&G, InnoCentive) | SUSTAINING (Apple, Toyota) |
| Problem not defined | BASIC RESEARCH (PARC, IBM) | DISRUPTIVE (Google, 3M) |
- Not only technology: process, thinking, doing, product. 4Ps of innovation: products, processes, people, place × incremental/distinctive/radical → 144 combinations. Whole-brain organisation: goals-, vision-, results-, people-driven.
- RENOVATION = incremental innovation (keeps core, least resistance; travel suitcase). Types: incremental, disruptive, radical, adjacent.
- KAIZEN (kai change + zen good): continuous small planned improvements (post-WWII quality circles). JUGAAD: frugal one-off fix — a starting point, NOT innovation (no planned end offering, may fail scrutiny). Improvisation and REVERSE ENGINEERING (copying) ≠ innovation.
- All inventions start creative; all inventions are innovations but not vice versa; not all creative solutions become innovations. Design ← invent ← innovate ← be creative.

# UNIT 9 — IPR (10 Sep)

# 9.1 Basics and types
- Intellectual = from the mind; IP = mind's output with value (own, sell, transfer); IPR = legal power to claim ownership and benefit. "IP = creation of wealth by the mind." IDEAS CANNOT BE PATENTED.
- Why IPR: creativity drives economy; knowledge = economic warfare; encourages research & investment; entrepreneurship; intellectual capital. Route to RECOGNITION, REWARD, RICHES.
- Entrepreneur must know: laws, ethics, authorities, forms/stages, legal support, employer/client agreements, transfer & sharing agreements, when to protect.
| Type | Protects | Act (India) |
| Patent | Novel, inventive, useful device/process | Patents Act 1970 |

# 9.2 Patents, copyright, design, trademarks, management
- Patent = contract inventor ↔ government: exclusive rights for full disclosure. Conditions: NOVELTY (not in global state of art), INVENTIVENESS (non-obvious to expert), USEFULNESS. Term 20 years; licensable. Procedure: file → examination & novelty search → accept/refuse → Gazette notification → opposition → grant.
- Not patentable: food/medicine/chemical substances (per slide), discovery of principle (Raman effect), new use/property of known substance, mere admixture, mere rearrangement of known devices, testing methods, agriculture/horticulture methods, medical treatment processes, atomic energy.
- COPYRIGHT: protects EXPRESSION not idea/content; automatic (registration advisable as proof); life + 60 years; covers sketches, drawings, software, UI, animation, typography, colour combinations, lectures, theses.
- DESIGN PATENT/registration: form & appearance only; iPhone shape = design patents, swipe-to-unlock = utility patent; DRAWINGS define boundaries → keep dated sketches.
- TRADEMARK: source indicator (word, phrase, symbol, design, domain, GI); logos are protectable.
- Managing IPR: case diary/sketch book — numbered pages, no overwriting, signed & witnessed; don't publish before filing; NDAs; disclose only what the form needs; legal advice. Know-how = trade secret; NDA before demos.
- CREATIVE COMMONS (2001, Lessig): "some rights reserved". Licences (most → least open): BY, BY-SA, BY-ND, BY-NC, BY-NC-SA, BY-NC-ND (BY attribution, SA share-alike, NC non-commercial, ND no derivatives).
- AI & IPR: only HUMANS are creators (AI artwork authorship rejected — DABUS). AI tools: The Lens, PatentPal, Clarivate, TrademarkNow, Markify, Turnitin, Copyleaks.
